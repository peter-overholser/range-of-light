## The race of one

Eric Schmidt, former CEO of Google and current CEO of [Relativity Space](https://www.relativityspace.com/), recently spoke on the All-In [podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EkuVqdj8O6E) about AI and the future.  At [one point](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EkuVqdj8O6E&t=229s), Schmidt contrasts American and Chinese approaches to the technology.  

In Schmidt's view, China is pursuing a pragmatic approach of using current AI technology to drive efficiencies across industry and society, while the US is hyperscaling in pursuit of general superintelligence.  

The race to develop nuclear arms is often invoked as an analogy for the race to build superintelligence. As with the nuclear race, it's not clear that there are multiple viable contenders.  Is a race with one participant a race or an exhibition?

Without existential competition driving the extreme resources we see devoted to this project of shrinking democratic support (and dubious financial wisdom), what compelling explanations remain?

## Infinite Money Glitch

In 2015, the market cap of Nvidia about a tenth of Intel's, and their products were best known to gamers and bitcoin fanatics.  A decade later, it's the world's most valuable company-- both a producer of computing infrastructure and major investor in the companies deploying and using this infrastructure.

Time and again, these investments have led to substantial gains in the market value of both sides, gains that dwarf the magnitude of the investments (though they are, themselves, astronomical).  Various commentators have pointed this out, most humorously [Dylan Patel](https://x.com/dylan522p/status/1970346183827783756):

## Incentives

The issue becomes much murkier, however, when incentives are brought into the picture.  Nvidia wants to sell as many chips as possible.  In fact, as an amoral corporation, it would most benefit (at least in the short term) if the most compute-heavy approaches to AI were pursued.  

Nothing has the potential to require more compute than the creation of a "digital god" (an explicit framing of much the American project).  Is there a possibility that the world's most valuable corporation is playing a part in setting this agenda, at the expense of more humane approaches?

We've already seen some evidence in this direction.  [DeepSeek r1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948) arrived as a bolt out of the blue, providing the capability of a frontier model at a small fraction of the resource demand.  Nothing in DeepSeek was beyond the reach of American researchers, but cultural and institutional forces pushed development away from this course.
